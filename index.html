Google Analytics is installed on more than half of all websites on the internet, and Meta's Pixel on roughly ten million. Both are offered to website operators at no cost. The reason is straightforward: the product being sold is not the analytics software but the behavioral data of everyone who visits the sites running it.

When this page loaded, four cookies were written to your browser. On a commercial site, these same mechanisms would be linking this visit to every prior session tied to your device, building a record that includes which articles you read, how far you scrolled, how long you paused, and what you searched before you arrived. That record is then packaged and sold, typically within milliseconds of your arrival, through automated auctions you cannot see and have no practical mechanism to opt out of.
